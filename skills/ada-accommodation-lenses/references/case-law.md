# Case law starter index (Corner 2)

Every entry here is tagged RECALL. It is a map of where to look, not authority to cite. Before any case goes into a message, a filing, or a brief:

1. Pull the opinion from the reporter or a free source (CourtListener, Google Scholar, the court's site).
2. Confirm the citation, the court, the year, and that the holding is what this index says.
3. Run a citator (KeyCite, Shepard's, or CourtListener's "cited by") for later history. Cases decided before 2009 may be limited by the ADA Amendments Act of 2008 on the question of who is disabled.
4. Prefer the user's own circuit. Supreme Court cases bind everywhere; another circuit's case is persuasive only.
5. Change the tag to VERIFIED (source, date checked, method) in the working notes, not in this file.

## Supreme Court (RECALL)

| Case | Point | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| *US Airways, Inc. v. Barnett*, 535 U.S. 391 (2002) | Reasonableness framework; an accommodation that conflicts with a bona fide seniority system is ordinarily not reasonable, but the employee may show special circumstances | Applies to reassignment disputes |
| *Cleveland v. Policy Management Systems Corp.*, 526 U.S. 795 (1999) | An SSDI claim of being "unable to work" does not automatically bar an ADA claim, but the employee must explain the apparent conflict | Corner 4 vulnerability: reconcile benefit forms with "qualified with accommodation" |
| *Chevron U.S.A. Inc. v. Echazabal*, 536 U.S. 73 (2002) | Direct-threat defense may extend to threat to the employee's own health, on an individualized assessment | Only if the employer raises safety |
| *Raytheon Co. v. Hernandez*, 540 U.S. 44 (2003) | Disparate treatment and disparate impact analyzed separately; neutral no-rehire policy | Narrow; disparate treatment framing |
| *Univ. of Texas Southwestern Medical Center v. Nassar*, 570 U.S. 338 (2013) | But-for causation for Title VII retaliation | Many circuits apply but-for to ADA retaliation; confirm for the circuit |
| *Burlington Northern & Santa Fe Railway Co. v. White*, 548 U.S. 53 (2006) | Retaliation covers actions that might dissuade a reasonable worker from complaining | Title VII; courts commonly apply it to 42 U.S.C. 12203 |
| *Sutton v. United Air Lines*, 527 U.S. 471 (1999); *Toyota Motor Mfg. v. Williams*, 534 U.S. 184 (2002) | Narrow "disability" readings | Superseded on those points by the ADA Amendments Act; do not rely on them for coverage |

## Circuit cases on the interactive process and essential functions (RECALL, check circuit)

| Case | Point |
|---|---|
| *Taylor v. Phoenixville School District*, 184 F.3d 296 (3d Cir. 1999) | Employer that fails to engage in good faith may be liable where an accommodation was possible; notice need not use formal words |
| *Humphrey v. Memorial Hospitals Ass'n*, 239 F.3d 1128 (9th Cir. 2001) | Duty to accommodate is continuing; employer that fails to engage in the interactive process faces liability where an accommodation would have been possible |
| *Fjellestad v. Pizza Hut of America, Inc.*, 188 F.3d 944 (8th Cir. 1999) | Failure to engage in the interactive process is evidence of bad faith; not a standalone claim; employee must still show an accommodation was possible |
| *EEOC v. Ford Motor Co.*, 782 F.3d 753 (6th Cir. 2015) (en banc) | Regular in-person attendance held an essential function on that record; telework not required. Fact-specific: the record on essential functions decides telework cases |
